Dye math

The recipe says 15% WOF and the sweater says nothing. Every natural dye quantity - mordant, dye matter, water - is a percentage of one number: the dry weight of your goods.

Weigh the fiber first

The problem it solves

Natural dye recipes are written in WOF - weight of goods - because the pot does not care about "one sweater." Fifteen percent alum for the mordant, twenty percent dye matter, a twenty-to-one bath: all of it scales from the dry weight of the fiber, and all of it goes wrong when that number lives in your head. Double the batch and the mordant comes up short; halve it and the dye comes out muddy from over-dosing.

Dye math turns the dry weight into grams and liters: the mordant dose and its bath, the dye matter at your chosen strength, and the water the pot actually needs for the fiber to swim.

What it does not do

The arithmetic is exact; the norms are not. Dye strength varies by plant, season, soil and water, and the dose bands here are flagged craft conventions. This measures nothing about your actual materials, and it gives no chemical-safety guidance - read the safety sheet for whatever goes in your pot.
